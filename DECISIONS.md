# Decisions log

| # | Decision | Alternative rejected | Why | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One holistic 0-4 score, anchored on 5 observable indicators | Five separately scored sub-dimensions | Recruiters need one decision-ready number, and five sub-scores from one short reply would be unreliable. The indicators still provide the explanation. | Less diagnostic detail. Sub-scores could be added later if reliability supports them. |
| 2 | LLM returns indicator-level evidence quotes, checked locally against the reply | Score plus free-text rationale only | A rationale can be invented, but a quote can be verified. Any quote not found in the reply sends the response to a person. | Longer output, and quotes may be short or partial. |
| 3 | Deterministic pre-checks (length, gibberish, no sentence structure, injection regex) before any model call | Let the LLM handle everything | Cheap, predictable and auditable, and it doesn't depend on model behaviour. Unscorable replies never get a number. | More human review volume. Thresholds need tuning on real data. |
| 4 | Human review for any flag or confidence < .6 | Fully automated cut-off | High-stakes selection: EU AI Act Art. 14 human oversight, UGESP defensibility. | Recruiter time. The routing rate has to be budgeted for (4 of 21 on the gold set in mock mode). |
| 5 | Rubric explicitly ignores minor spelling and regional English | Score spelling as part of writing quality | The construct is customer focus, not standard-English correctness. Penalising dialect risks adverse impact that the job doesn't justify. | A client may want spelling. That would be a separate, explicit, job-analysed criterion. |
| 6 | Candidate text delimited as data; model told to flag, not follow, embedded instructions; regex backstop | Trust the model | Candidates have an incentive to game the scorer. | Some false flags from replies that mention a "rubric". |
| 7 | Mock mode with transparent keyword rules, not canned outputs | Hard-coded fake scores | A reviewer can run it with no key, and it gives a real baseline that shows its own failure (keyword stuffing). | Mock numbers say nothing about LLM accuracy. This is stated wherever they appear. |
| 8 | Score the written reply only; no emotion, personality or "AI-written" inference used in the score | Detect AI-generated text and penalise it | AI-text detectors are unreliable and biased against non-native writers. Emotion inference at work is prohibited under the EU AI Act. | Integrity relies on proctoring, timing and follow-up interview, not on detection. |
| 9 | Python + Streamlit, single folder, effort=low, structured outputs | R Shiny; free-text JSON parsing | Fastest path to a runnable demo; structured outputs remove most parse failures. | Live path not exercised in this session (no key). |
| 10 | v0.2: added `no_sentence_structure` guard after v0.1 eval showed keyword stuffing scored 4 | Leave as a known limit | A cheap, construct-agnostic guard. Both runs are reported. | It was tuned on a test case, so it needs fresh adversarial cases to confirm. |
